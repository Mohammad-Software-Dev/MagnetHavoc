# Magnet Havoc — Game Vision

## Elevator pitch

**Magnet Havoc** is a competitive 3D mobile arena game for 4–8 players. Players control small magnetic fighters in compact floating arenas, using movement, dashes, attraction, and repulsion to steal objectives, weaponize physics props, disrupt rivals, and knock opponents out of the arena. Matches are short, readable, expressive, and designed to create rematch-worthy stories.

The fantasy is not “military combat.” It is a futuristic televised sport where magnetic athletes turn industrial arenas into controlled chaos.

## Player fantasy

The player should feel like a clever, agile troublemaker who can manipulate the arena itself. The best moments come from timing and spatial awareness rather than memorizing complex ability kits:

- Pull the objective away just before a rival reaches it.
- Drag a metal crate into position, then blast it across the arena.
- Repel an opponent mid-dash and send them off a platform.
- Recover from near elimination with a last-second dash.
- Use a hazard against someone without directly touching them.
- Win by a tiny margin and immediately request a rematch.

## Audience

Primary audience:
- Mobile players who enjoy short competitive sessions.
- Players of party games, arena brawlers, social PvP, and light sports games.
- Friend groups who want something understandable within one match.
- Players who like mastery without long control tutorials.

Secondary audience:
- Casual players attracted by funny physics clips.
- Competitive players motivated by ranked ladders and mastery.
- Content creators looking for short, surprising moments.

The game should be enjoyable for a first-time player but allow hundreds of hours of improvement through movement, timing, prediction, arena knowledge, and social competition.

## Product pillars

### 1. Movement first
Movement is never a transport chore. Acceleration, turning, dashing, impacts, edge recovery, and animation must feel satisfying before any progression system exists.

### 2. Magnetism is the signature
The core mechanic is polarity: **pull things toward you; push things away from you**. It must affect the objective, players, props, and selected hazards. New content should deepen this mechanic rather than replace it.

### 3. Short, emotional matches
Target match length is 90–150 seconds. A match should contain multiple momentum swings and end with a clear emotional beat: relief, laughter, frustration, revenge, or pride.

### 4. Social replayability
The result screen is not the end; it is a bridge to the next match. Rematch, add friend, party up, rivalry, crew progress, and shareable results should be prominent.

### 5. Competitive integrity
Ranked outcomes should be determined primarily by skill. Paid power is prohibited. Gameplay-affecting options, if introduced, must be sidegrades available through fair play.

### 6. Clip readability
A non-player watching five seconds of footage should understand the action: one character pulls, another is launched, an objective changes hands, or a hazard causes a knockout.

### 7. Small-team discipline
The core game must work with one map, one avatar rig, one mode, and placeholder art. Content quantity cannot hide a weak core loop.

## Differentiation

Magnet Havoc sits between a party game and an arena brawler. Its differentiation is that players do not primarily attack with guns, swords, or hero abilities. They manipulate **forces and objects**. The arena is part of the weapon set.

The design deliberately avoids:
- 20+ launch characters with unique combat kits.
- Complex inventory or loot during a match.
- Long battle royale sessions.
- Aim-heavy shooter controls.
- Stat upgrades that determine PvP outcomes.
- Large maps requiring minutes of traversal.

## Emotional target

A successful session should generate sentences like:
- “How did you survive that?”
- “You hit me with my own crate.”
- “One more game.”
- “Invite me.”
- “I was one point away.”

The strongest north-star sentence is:

> **I understood it immediately, something ridiculous happened, and I want a rematch.**

## Platform and presentation

- Primary: iOS and Android phones/tablets.
- Orientation: landscape.
- Visual style: stylized 3D sci-fi / toy-like arena sport.
- Session pattern: 2–4 minute burst or 15–25 minute social session.
- Business model: free-to-play, cosmetic-first.

## Success definition

The project is successful at prototype stage if four people with placeholder characters voluntarily keep rematching because the movement and push/pull interactions are funny and competitive. If that does not happen, no amount of progression, cosmetics, lore, or live operations should be built to compensate.
