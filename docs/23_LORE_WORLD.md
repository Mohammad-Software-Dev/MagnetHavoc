# Lore and World

## Lore philosophy

Lore supports identity and seasonal themes but must never obstruct immediate gameplay comprehension. A player can enjoy Magnet Havoc without reading a single paragraph of story.

## The world

The floating metropolis **Gridspire** is powered by Flux: a strange energy that can manipulate magnetic forces at enormous scale. The city's maintenance robots originally used Flux technology to move cargo, repair transit rails, and stabilize floating infrastructure.

Then somebody discovered that industrial magnet rigs were extremely entertaining when used in an arena.

The informal competitions became a citywide phenomenon. Old cargo yards, energy plants, rooftops, and test platforms were rebuilt into broadcast stages. Competitors customized their chassis, formed local crews, and fought for control of portable **Flux Cores**.

The sanctioned competition is now called the **Havoc League**.

## Players: Mags

Competitors are nicknamed **Mags**. Some are purpose-built sports machines; others are retrofitted maintenance units, scrapyard creations, ceremonial bots, or eccentric custom chassis.

This allows huge cosmetic variety without requiring separate species/class rules.

## Flux Core

A Flux Core is a stabilized portable energy unit. In-world, teams compete to capture and charge league scoring towers with them. In gameplay, the Core is the central objective.

## Arenas

### Skyforge Station
An old aerial assembly platform converted into the league's most famous beginner-friendly stadium.

### Scrapworks Yard
A recycling district whose owners realized paying spectators were more profitable than ordinary scrap processing.

### Flux Foundry
An active energy plant with polarity rails still embedded in the floor. League engineers insist it is “perfectly safe.”

### Neon Rooftops
A pirate-style rooftop circuit later approved as an official event after becoming wildly popular.

## Crews and districts

Gridspire is divided into districts that compete for prestige and energy allocation. Player Crews adopt district-style identities, explaining weekly Crew competitions.

## Tone

- Playful, not grim.
- Rivalry without war.
- Mechanical slapstick.
- Sports broadcast energy.
- Characters can be cocky, weird, proud, or silly.

No elaborate chosen-one narrative is needed.

## Announcer voice

A league announcer can provide short energetic lines:
- “CORE LOOSE!”
- “OVERLOAD!”
- “LAST TEN!”
- “WHAT A LAUNCH!”
- “VICTORY!”

Keep lines short and localizable.
